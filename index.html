📘 Blackjack Rules & Table Guide

A simple reference for the rules used in this trainer. Open any section for an example.

🎯 Goal of Blackjack
Beat the dealer without going over 21. You win by finishing with a higher total than the dealer, or when the dealer busts.
10♠7♥
Example: 10 + 7 = 17. You have not busted because the total is 21 or less.
🃏 Card Values & Aces
Cards 2–10 are worth their number. J, Q and K are worth 10. An Ace is worth 11 when possible, but becomes 1 when 11 would make the hand bust.
A♠6♦
A + 6 is soft 17. The Ace can still change from 11 to 1 if another card is drawn.
⭐ Natural Blackjack
A natural blackjack is an Ace plus any 10-value card as your first two cards. In this trainer it pays 3:2. If the dealer also has a natural blackjack, the result is a push.
A♣K♥
➕ Hit / ✋ Stand
Hit means take another card. Stand means keep your current total and end your turn.
9♣7♦
Example: with 16 you may hit or stand depending on the dealer card and the strategy situation.
×2 Double Down
Double your original wager, receive exactly one more card, then your hand automatically stands. It is normally available only on your first two cards, subject to table rules.
5♠6♥K♣
Example: 5 + 6 = 11, double, receive one final card.
↔️ Split
When your first two cards form a pair and the table rules allow it, you can split them into two separate hands. A second wager equal to the first is placed, and each hand is then played separately.
8♠8♥
After the split, each 8 starts a new hand. Rules for resplitting or split Aces can vary by casino.
🏳️ Surrender
With late surrender, when the option is available, you give up the hand before taking another card and receive half of your original wager back. The other half is lost. Not every table offers surrender.
$20 wager → surrender → $10 returned, $10 lost.
Important: if surrender is available, it is checked before the Hit/Stand decision. Some Stage 7 count drills focus specifically on the Hit/Stand index layer.
🛡️ Insurance
Insurance is offered when the dealer shows an Ace. It is a separate side bet of up to half your original wager. If the dealer has blackjack, insurance pays 2:1; otherwise the insurance bet loses.
A♦?
Basic Strategy normally declines insurance. In this Hi-Lo training model, a common count benchmark is to take it around True Count +3 or higher.
🤵 Dealer Rules — S17
In this trainer the dealer hits 16 or less and stands on every 17 or higher, including soft 17 (S17). The dealer does not choose strategically; the rule determines every draw.
A♠6♥
Here the dealer has soft 17 and stands because this trainer uses S17.
☁️ Soft Hand vs 🧱 Hard Hand
A soft hand contains an Ace currently counted as 11, so one extra card cannot immediately bust the hand. A hard hand has no Ace counted as 11.
A♣6♦10♠6♥
A+6 is soft 17. 10+6 is hard 16.
✅ Win / ❌ Loss / 💥 Bust / 🤝 Push
Win: your final total beats the dealer, or the dealer busts.
Loss: the dealer finishes higher without busting, or you bust first.
Bust: a total above 21; that hand loses immediately.
Push: equal final totals; your original wager is returned.
🧠 Basic Strategy vs Card Counting
Basic Strategy is the default. Card counting does not replace it. The count mainly helps estimate when the remaining shoe is richer in high cards, size a training bet, and make a small number of learned deviations.
Example: 12 vs 2 is normally HIT in the Hit/Stand baseline. In the Hi-Lo index set used by this course, it changes to STAND at about True Count +3 or higher.
To calculate a useful True Count at a real table, you also need to estimate how many decks remain in the shoe. Use the Deck Estimation Trainer from Home to practice this visually.

Stage 1 — Quick Lesson

What is Hi-Lo?

Hi-Lo assigns every card one of three values. You start at 0 and update the Running Count as cards appear.

+1
2, 3, 4, 5, 6
0
7, 8, 9
−1
10, J, Q, K, A
Example:
5 = +1
8 = 0
K = −1

A complete 52-card deck is balanced. If every card is counted correctly, the final count returns to 0.

Stage 7 — Quick Lesson

Using the Count at the Table

Up to now you learned how to keep a Running Count and convert it to a True Count. This stage teaches what those numbers actually change during a blackjack game.

1. Keep the Running Count continuously
Never restart after each hand. Keep counting every exposed card until the shoe is shuffled.

2. Convert to True Count before using the information
True Count = Running Count ÷ decks remaining.

3. Use the True Count to choose a training bet size
A unit means your base bet. If 1 unit = $10, then the learning ramp looks like this:

TC ≤ 0 → 1 unit = $10
TC +1 → 2 units = $20
TC +2 → 4 units = $40
TC +3 → 6 units = $60
TC +4 or higher → 8 units = $80

This is a learning bet ramp used inside the simulator, not a universal casino rule or a promise of profit.

Count information does not mean changing every blackjack decision. Most hands still follow Basic Strategy. A few well-known count-aware deviations are introduced here, including Insurance around True Count +3 or higher, 16 vs 10 at TC 0+, 15 vs 10 at TC +4, 12 vs 3 at TC +2, and 12 vs 2 at TC +3.

This stage has three parts:
Part A — Count → Bet
Part B — Count → Decision
Part C — Full Table Integration

Stage 8 — Quick Lesson

Counting a Full Blackjack Table

At a busy table, many more cards are exposed each round. Your job is still the same: keep one Running Count for every exposed card — yours, the dealer's, and every other player's cards.

What changes with more players?

• More cards appear before your decision, so the Running Count can move quickly.
• Do not keep a separate count for each player — there is only one count for the whole shoe.
• Count cards in a steady order around the table instead of jumping randomly between hands.
• Dealer hole cards are counted only when they are revealed.

A simple rhythm is: scan each exposed hand from left to right, cancel +1 and −1 cards when possible, then carry the final Running Count into the next round.

What you will do in this stage

1. Start from the Running Count shown.
2. Count every exposed card on a multi-player table.
3. Enter the new Running Count.
4. Convert it to the True Count using the decks remaining.

For this level, decks remaining are shown as whole decks, and True Count answers are built around whole numbers or half steps.
